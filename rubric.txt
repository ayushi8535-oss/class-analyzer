7. AUDIT PHILOSOPHY
Evidence-based, observable, reproducible. No instinct, personality judgements, or assumptions.
For every important finding:
Criterion → Observable Behaviour → Timestamp → Impact
Example:

8. WHAT COUNTS AS EVIDENCE
Strong evidence: exact tutor/student behaviour, questions asked, responses given, explanations, attempts, corrections, adaptations, explicit transitions, observable timing, parent statements, AC statements.
Do not rely on vague conclusions ("tutor was not engaging"). Identify the observable behaviour instead ("Between 17:20–19:05, tutor explains continuously without asking the student to attempt or explain").
________________________________________
9. WHAT AI MUST NOT INFER
Never infer internal states or intentions without direct evidence. Do not say "tutor was nervous / didn't care / was lazy," "student was bored," "parent was unhappy," etc. Describe the observable behaviour instead.

DEMO SESSION SUCCESS & AUDIT FRAMEWORK
Purpose
The framework defines what a successful demo session looks like and provides a consistent method for auditing tutor performance.
The audit should answer three questions:
1.	What did the tutor do?
2.	What evidence shows that they did it?
3.	What should the tutor do differently next time?
Core principle: Audit observable tutor behaviour, not intent or impression.
1. SCORING FRAMEWORK
Each parameter is scored on a 0–2 scale.
Score	Meaning
0 — Not Met	Expected behaviour was absent or the opposite behaviour was observed.
1 — Partially Met	Behaviour occurred but was inconsistent, incomplete, or insufficient.
2 — Met	Behaviour was clearly demonstrated with sufficient observable evidence.
N/A	Only when the parameter genuinely could not apply.
Important
A score must be supported by evidence.
Every scored parameter should have:
Timestamp + What happened + Why it matters
Example:
Teaching – Student Participation: 1/2
 14:32 — Tutor introduces an example and begins solving it without first asking the student to attempt it.
 16:08 — Tutor gives the student an opportunity to solve the next example.
 Finding: Student participation was present but inconsistent.

2. SESSION STRUCTURE
The demo is evaluated across five stages:
01 — Rapport & Context
Understand the student
02 — Diagnostic
Understand what the student knows and how they think
03 — Teaching
Address the gap and enable the student to apply
04 — Summary
Establish what changed
05 — Parent Interaction
Communicate evidence, progress and next steps

3. RAPPORT & CONTEXT
Objective
The tutor establishes a comfortable, professional relationship with the student and gathers enough context to understand who the student is, what they need, and what they are trying to achieve.
Rapport is not about completing a fixed list of introductory questions. It is about the quality and relevance of the interaction.
________________________________________
3.1 Build a Genuine Connection
Success criteria
The tutor creates a natural two-way interaction that helps the student feel comfortable and willing to engage in the session.
The tutor:
●	Initiates conversation naturally rather than immediately moving into instruction.
●	Shows genuine interest in the student's responses.
●	Builds on what the student says instead of moving mechanically from one question to another.
●	Uses an appropriate and friendly tone.
●	Avoids making the interaction feel scripted or like an interview.
●	Creates an environment where the student feels comfortable responding and asking questions.
Observable evidence
Examples may include:
"What do you enjoy doing outside school?"
"You mentioned you play football — what position do you play?"
"How do you usually find maths at school?"
The specific question is not what is being assessed. The quality of the interaction is.
Audit
0: Tutor makes little or no meaningful attempt to establish a connection and moves directly into the academic task.
1: Tutor attempts to build rapport, but the interaction feels brief, scripted or transactional.
2: Tutor creates a natural two-way interaction, responds meaningfully to the student and establishes a comfortable connection.
Key evidence: Is the tutor genuinely interacting with the student, or simply completing an introduction?
________________________________________
3.2 Understand the Student's Context
Success criteria
The tutor makes an effort to understand why the student is attending the session and what they need from it.
The tutor may explore relevant areas such as:
●	Learning goals
●	Current challenges
●	Upcoming exams or assessments
●	Topics being studied
●	Previous learning experience
●	Confidence level
●	Areas the student finds easy or difficult
●	Motivation or expectations
The tutor does not need to ask about every area. The focus is on identifying relevant context that can inform the session.
Audit
0: Tutor makes no meaningful attempt to understand the student's learning context.
1: Tutor establishes basic context, but the questions remain generic or are not explored enough to understand the student's needs.
2: Tutor establishes specific, relevant context that gives them a clear understanding of the student's needs or goals.
Key evidence: Does the tutor understand what this particular student needs from the session?
________________________________________
3.3 Use Student Context
Success criteria
The tutor uses information gathered from the student to make the session more relevant or personalised.
This may be reflected in:
●	Choice of examples
●	Choice of questions
●	Level of difficulty
●	Explanation style
●	Teaching approach
●	Pace
●	References to the student's goals
●	Connections to the student's existing experience
Personalisation does not require forced references to the student's interests. It should be natural and relevant to the learning experience.
Audit
0: Tutor gathers or could have gathered context but the session remains completely generic.
1: Tutor references something the student shared, but its use is superficial or has little impact on the session.
2: Tutor meaningfully incorporates relevant student context into the teaching or interaction.
Key evidence: Can we identify something the tutor learned about the student that subsequently changed or informed the session?
________________________________________
Overall principle
Rapport & Context should progress as:
Connect → Understand → Use
The tutor should not be rewarded simply for asking questions.
The strongest evidence is:
Tutor engages naturally → learns something meaningful about the student → uses that understanding to make the session more relevant.

________________________________________
4. DIAGNOSTIC
Objective
The tutor uses the beginning of the academic interaction to understand the student's current knowledge, reasoning and learning gaps before providing significant instruction.
Diagnostic guardrail
Target: ≤5 minutes
The diagnostic should generate enough evidence to understand the student's starting point without becoming a teaching segment.
________________________________________
4.1 Give the Student an Opportunity to Think
Success criteria
The tutor gives the student an opportunity to engage with the question before explaining, correcting or revealing the approach.
The tutor allows the student to:
●	Read or interpret the question
●	Identify relevant information
●	Explain what they understand
●	Describe what needs to be found
●	Attempt an approach
●	Share their initial reasoning
The tutor should avoid immediately taking over the thinking process.
Observable evidence
The tutor:
●	Presents a question and gives the student time to process it.
●	Asks the student what they understand before explaining.
●	Waits for the student's attempt or reasoning.
●	Avoids giving away the method too early.
●	Allows the student to make an attempt, even if imperfect.
Audit
0: Tutor immediately explains, solves or reveals the approach without giving the student a meaningful opportunity to think.
1: Tutor gives the student some opportunity to respond but intervenes quickly or provides significant direction before the student can demonstrate their thinking.
2: Tutor clearly gives the student space to interpret and attempt the question before providing instruction.
Key evidence: Did the tutor allow the student to think before teaching?
________________________________________
4.2 Probe Student Reasoning
Success criteria
The tutor uses questioning to understand how the student is thinking, not simply whether they have the correct answer.
The tutor probes relevant aspects of the student's:
●	Interpretation
●	Reasoning
●	Approach
●	Decision-making
●	Understanding
●	Mistakes or misconceptions
The questions should allow the student to explain rather than simply produce a yes/no or one-word answer.
Observable evidence
The tutor may:
●	Ask the student to explain their approach.
●	Ask why they selected a method.
●	Ask how they arrived at an answer.
●	Ask what they think should happen next.
●	Ask the student to explain an incorrect answer.
●	Follow up on an unexpected response.
The exact wording is not important.
Audit
0: Tutor does not meaningfully probe the student's reasoning.
1: Tutor asks some questions, but they are mostly closed, leading, or focused only on obtaining an answer.
2: Tutor uses relevant open-ended questioning to understand the student's reasoning and thinking.
Key evidence: Is the tutor trying to understand the student's thinking, or only checking whether they got the answer right?
________________________________________
4.3 Identify the Learning Gap
Success criteria
The tutor uses evidence from the student's responses to identify what is actually causing the difficulty.
The tutor distinguishes between:
●	Not knowing the concept
●	Misunderstanding the concept
●	Choosing the wrong method
●	Difficulty interpreting the question
●	Calculation/accuracy errors
●	Difficulty applying knowledge
●	Lack of independent reasoning
●	Confidence or hesitation
The identified gap should influence what happens next.
Observable evidence
Strong evidence follows this sequence:
Student response → Tutor recognises specific issue → Tutor adjusts teaching
For example:
Student understands the formula but cannot identify when to use it → tutor focuses on recognising the appropriate method rather than reteaching the formula.
Audit
0: Tutor does not establish a meaningful learning gap.
1: Tutor recognises that the student is struggling, but the specific reason is unclear or the subsequent teaching is only loosely connected to the evidence.
2: Tutor identifies a specific learning need from the student's response and uses it to determine what to teach next.
Key evidence: Can the auditor identify what the tutor learned about the student's gap and how it affected the teaching?
________________________________________
Diagnostic Principle
Think → Probe → Identify
The tutor should not be rewarded simply for asking diagnostic questions.
The strongest evidence is:
Student attempts → Tutor investigates thinking → Tutor identifies the underlying gap → Teaching responds to that gap.


5. TEACHING
Objective
The tutor delivers an engaging, student-centred learning experience where the student actively participates, the tutor responds to the student's needs, and the student demonstrates understanding through application.
________________________________________
5.1 Student Involvement
Success criteria
The student is actively involved in the learning rather than passively listening to the tutor.
The tutor creates regular opportunities for the student to:
●	Share what they already know or think
●	Answer questions
●	Interpret questions or concepts
●	Attempt problems
●	Explain their reasoning
●	Predict answers
●	Ask questions
●	Solve or apply concepts independently
The tutor should not consistently do the thinking or solving on the student's behalf.
Audit
0: Tutor predominantly lectures, explains or solves, with minimal student involvement.
1: Student participates occasionally, but involvement is inconsistent and the tutor still does most of the thinking/doing.
2: Student is consistently involved through meaningful questioning, thinking, attempting, explaining and applying.
Key evidence: Is the tutor actively involving the student in the session rather than lecturing?________________________________________
5.2 Engagement & Energy
Success criteria
The session is interactive, engaging and appropriately paced for the student.
The tutor:
●	Maintains interaction rather than delivering a one-way lecture
●	Uses questions and conversation to maintain attention
●	Responds naturally to the student's answers and reactions
●	Varies the interaction where appropriate
●	Maintains an appropriate pace
●	Encourages the student to participate and attempt answers
●	Creates a comfortable environment where the student is willing to engage
Audit
0: Session is largely passive or lecture-driven, with minimal interaction or visible student engagement.
1: Session is reasonably interactive, but engagement is inconsistent or heavily dependent on tutor-led explanation.
2: Session is consistently interactive and appropriately paced, with clear evidence that the student is engaged and responsive.
Key evidence: Does the session feel like an engaging learning experience, or like the tutor talking at the student?
________________________________________
5.3 Adaptability
Success criteria
The tutor recognises what the student needs and adjusts their teaching approach accordingly.
Student signals that may require adaptation
●	Confusion
●	Hesitation or difficulty responding
●	Repeated mistakes
●	Unexpectedly strong performance
●	Student indicating that something is easy or difficult
●	Slow learning pace / requiring more processing time
●	Low interest or lack of enjoyment in the subject
●	Very weak foundational knowledge
●	Preference for visual or alternative ways of learning
Tutor adjustments may include
●	Slowing down or increasing the pace
●	Breaking a concept into smaller steps
●	Providing additional scaffolding
●	Removing scaffolding when the student is ready
●	Using a different explanation
●	Using a visual, diagram, model or real-life analogy
●	Changing the difficulty of questions
●	Revisiting foundational concepts
●	Giving additional thinking time
●	Changing from explanation to guided questioning
●	Using examples connected to the student's interests
●	Changing the mode of practice or application
Audit
0: Tutor does not respond appropriately to clear evidence that the student needs a different approach.
1: Tutor recognises the student's signal and makes a limited or superficial adjustment.
2: Tutor clearly identifies what the student needs and meaningfully changes their teaching approach.
Key evidence: Student Signal → Tutor Adjustment.
Important: A change in activity by itself is not adaptability. The adjustment must be connected to something the tutor observes about the student.
________________________________________
5.4 Understanding & Application
Success criteria
The tutor verifies whether the student can actually demonstrate and apply what was taught.
Evidence may include the student:
●	Solving a new question
●	Applying the method independently
●	Explaining the concept
●	Explaining their reasoning
●	Applying the concept in a new context
Audit
0: Tutor explains the concept and moves on without meaningfully checking understanding.
1: Tutor checks understanding, but evidence is limited or the student remains heavily dependent on tutor support.
2: Student independently demonstrates understanding through explanation, reasoning or application.
Important: "Do you understand?" alone is not sufficient evidence of understanding.
________________________________________
5.5 Subject Knowledge & Explanation Accuracy
Success criteria
The tutor demonstrates accurate subject knowledge and explains concepts clearly at an appropriate level for the student.
The tutor:
●	Provides correct explanations and solutions
●	Uses accurate terminology
●	Gives relevant examples
●	Explains reasoning clearly
●	Identifies and addresses misconceptions correctly
●	Answers relevant student questions accurately
Audit
0: Tutor provides a materially incorrect or misleading explanation/solution, reinforces a misconception, or is unable to correctly address a relevant student question.
1: Explanation is broadly correct but contains minor inaccuracies, unclear reasoning, inappropriate terminology, or is unnecessarily complex for the student's level.
2: Tutor consistently provides accurate, clear and logically sound explanations appropriate to the student's level.

________________________________________
6. SUMMARY & STUDENT REFLECTION
Objective
The tutor closes the teaching segment by giving the student an opportunity to reflect on their learning, confidence and experience, while checking whether the student can independently demonstrate what they have learned.
The summary should be student-led, rather than becoming another tutor explanation.
________________________________________
6.1 Student Reflection & Confidence
Success criteria
The tutor gives the student an opportunity to reflect on the session by asking open-ended questions about:
●	What they understood or learned.
●	What they found easy or difficult.
●	How confident they feel after the session.
●	Whether anything still feels unclear.
●	What they feel they need more practice with.
The tutor should listen to the student's response and use it to understand how the student is feeling about the learning.
Observable evidence
Examples:
"What do you feel you understand better now?"
"What part was still difficult for you?"
"How confident do you feel about solving this on your own now?"
"Is there anything you'd like us to practise again?"
Audit
0 — Not Met: Tutor does not meaningfully invite the student to reflect on their learning or confidence.
1 — Partially Met: Tutor asks for a reflection, but questions are largely closed, generic, or the tutor quickly moves on without exploring the response.
2 — Met: Tutor creates a genuine opportunity for the student to reflect on their learning, difficulty and confidence using open-ended questions.
Key evidence: Did the student get the opportunity to articulate their own learning and confidence, rather than the tutor simply telling them how they performed?

6.2 Connect Today's Learning to What's Next
Success criteria
The tutor uses the end of the session to connect what the student learned today with what they will work towards in upcoming sessions.
The tutor should:
●	Ask the student what they learned or understood from the session.
●	Briefly acknowledge the student's response.
●	Give the student a sense of how today's learning will build into upcoming classes.
●	Where relevant, connect this back to the goal, concern or context shared by the student at the beginning of the session.
●	Help the student leave with a clear and positive sense of what they will work towards next.
The tutor does not need to explain the entire study plan or provide a detailed academic strategy at this stage.
Observable evidence
For example:
"What do you feel you learned today?"
After the student responds:
"Exactly. Today we covered the basics of the Cosine Rule so you could get comfortable with the concept. In the upcoming classes, we'll go much deeper into applying it to different types of questions, especially based on what you're currently doing at school."
Or, where a student has shared a specific goal:
"You mentioned at the beginning that you want to feel more confident with exam-style questions. Today we started with the fundamentals, and in the upcoming sessions we'll build from this into more challenging questions so you can work towards that goal."
Audit
0 — Not Met: Tutor ends the session without giving the student an opportunity to reflect or without connecting today's session to what comes next.
1 — Partially Met: Tutor asks the student about their learning or mentions what comes next, but the connection between today's session and the student's broader context or goal is limited.
2 — Met: Tutor gives the student an opportunity to reflect and creates a clear, relevant connection between what was learned today, the student's goal/context, and what they will work towards in upcoming sessions.
Key evidence: Does the ending connect where the conversation started → what the student learned today → where the student is going next?

Reflect → Connect → Look Ahead
The tutor should:
Let the student reflect on today's learning → connect it back to their goal or context → give them something meaningful to look forward to in the upcoming sessions.

7.2 Communicate Evidence-Based Personalised Feedback
Success criteria
The tutor explains what they personally observed about the student, using evidence from the session and the post-session report.
The tutor should interpret the evidence rather than simply read from the product or report.
Relevant evidence may include:
●	Diagnostic performance / starting point
●	Accuracy
●	Questions attempted
●	Level or difficulty reached
●	Independence or prompting required
●	Specific mistakes or misconceptions
●	Application of the concept
●	Confidence or engagement
●	Change in performance during the session
The tutor does not need to mention every metric. They should select the most relevant evidence to explain the student's performance.
Observable evidence
Instead of:
"The report shows 20% accuracy and Level 4."
The tutor should explain what that means:
"He was able to reach Level 4 questions, which shows he can engage with higher-level problems, but the 20% accuracy tells us that his conceptual understanding still needs strengthening before we can expect consistent independent performance."
Audit
0 — Not Met: Feedback is absent, generic, or primarily consists of reading/repeating information from the product.
1 — Partially Met: Tutor communicates some student-specific observations or data, but provides limited interpretation or evidence.
2 — Met: Tutor uses relevant session evidence to give clear, personalised feedback and explains what the evidence means academically.
Key evidence: Is the tutor interpreting the student's performance, or simply reading the report?

7.4 Explain the Plan of Action & Study Plan
Success criteria
The tutor explains what will happen next to address the identified gap and uses the study plan to make the future learning journey tangible.
The plan should follow:
Gap → Intervention → Expected Outcome
The tutor should:
●	Explain what will be worked on.
●	Explain how the identified gap will be addressed.
●	Explain how the difficulty or level of application will progress where relevant.
●	Explain the expected outcome.
●	Walk through the relevant upcoming topics or sessions in the study plan.
●	Connect the study plan back to the student's gap and goal.
The tutor should not simply screen-share or read the study plan.
Observable evidence
For example:

We'll first strengthen the underlying concept, then move into mixed and exam-style questions, gradually reducing support so the student can solve them independently. 1 — Partially Met: Tutor gives a generic plan or shows/reads the study plan without clearly explaining how it addresses the student's need.
2 — Met: Tutor clearly explains the Gap → Intervention → Expected Outcome and uses the study plan to show how this will translate into the student's upcoming learning journey.
Key evidence: Does the parent understand what the student needs, what the tutor will do about it, and how the study plan supports that journey?

DEMO SESSION AUDIT
ZTP & HYGIENE NON-NEGOTIABLES
Purpose: Define objective, auditable standards for tutor conduct, professionalism and demo readiness.
ZTP — ZERO TOLERANCE PARAMETERS  |  Classification and Scoring
Audit Rule: Any confirmed ZTP violation constitutes an automatic NON-SATISFACTORY outcome and escalation, irrespective of the tutor’s academic score. Record the behaviour and timestamp.
#	Audit Parameter	PASS — Observable Standard	FAIL — Trigger
1	Student Dignity & Psychological Safety	Tutor maintains a supportive environment in which the student can answer, make mistakes and ask questions without fear of embarrassment or judgement.	Humiliating, intimidating, ridiculing, shaming or visibly uncomfortable behaviour toward the student.
2	Response to Student Uncertainty & Errors	Tutor responds constructively when the student says “I don't know,” makes an error, or is unable to answer; uses the response diagnostically and guides appropriately.	Mocking, shaming, dismissing, expressing frustration toward, or negatively labelling the student for uncertainty or an error.
3	Professional Conduct & Undivided Attention	Tutor remains fully engaged with the student and conducts the session without unrelated activity.	Eating/chewing, phone use, unrelated browsing/work, household/personal tasks, or unrelated conversations during the demo.






 
HYGIENE & DEMO READINESS  |  Classification and Scoring
Audit Rule: Each criterion is assessed independently. More than 2 failed criteria = WARNING. Hygiene failures remain applicable regardless of the academic/demo score.
#	Audit Parameter	PASS — Observable Standard	FAIL — Trigger
1	Punctuality & Session Preparedness	Tutor joins at the scheduled time and is ready to commence with required materials, platform and session setup in place.	Tutor joins late, is not ready to begin, or spends significant session time completing basic preparation.
2	Internet, Audio/Video & Technical Stability	Audio, video, internet and screen-sharing remain sufficiently stable to support uninterrupted delivery.	Repeated or significant technical disruptions materially interrupt the session or student experience.
3	Professional Appearance, Background & Lighting	Tutor presents an appropriate professional appearance; face is clearly visible; lighting and background are suitable for a formal student-facing session.	Poor visibility, inadequate lighting, visibly inappropriate/unprofessional appearance, or distracting/inappropriate background.
4	Environment & Session Focus	Tutor operates from a quiet, controlled environment and remains focused on the student throughout.	Avoidable background disruption, talking to others, leaving for non-emergency reasons, multitasking, eating/chewing, or other environmental distractions affecting delivery.
5	Warm, Engaging & Approachable Presence	Tutor demonstrates an appropriate level of warmth through a welcoming tone, positive facial expression/body language, active listening and an approachable manner that encourages participation.	Tutor consistently demonstrates a cold, irritated, disengaged, dismissive or unapproachable manner that negatively affects student interaction.

AUDITOR EVIDENCE STANDARD
For every FAIL, record: Criterion → Observable behaviour → Timestamp → Impact, where applicable.
Example: Environment & Focus — FAIL | 11:46–12:47 — Tutor leaves the session to attend to a door interruption, resulting in a prolonged break in the demo.
Important distinction: “I don't know” is not itself a ZTP. The ZTP applies to the tutor’s response. Eating/chewing is a hygiene/session-discipline failure; persistent or clearly unprofessional eating/chewing may also be treated as a ZTP under Professional Conduct.
